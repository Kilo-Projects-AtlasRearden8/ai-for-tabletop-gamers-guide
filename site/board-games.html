Using Free AI with Board Games

This guide focuses heavily on tabletop roleplaying games, but free AI is just as helpful at the board game table. AI can explain rules in plain language, help you teach new players, suggest setup and teardown checklists, brainstorm solo or variant play, and help track scores — all without replacing the fun of playing with friends.

As with roleplaying games, AI is a helper, not a rules authority. Always check against the game's rulebook, and never treat AI output as an official ruling.

Copy-and-paste prompt: explain a board game rule

I play the board game [name of game]. Please explain the rule for [specific rule, e.g. "how scoring works" or "what happens when the deck runs out"] in plain language, as if I am teaching it to someone for the first time. Include:

  1. What the rule does
  2. When to use it
  3. A simple example with made-up turns or scores

Do not quote the rulebook word for word. Mark anything you are unsure about with [unsure].

Copy-and-paste prompt: teach new players

I am teaching [name of game] to new players who have never played it before. Please give me:

  1. A one-sentence "elevator pitch" for what the game is about
  2. The goal of the game (how to win)
  3. A short, ordered list of the main phases of play (4-6 steps)
  4. Two or three common mistakes new players make
  5. One tip to make the first game go smoothly

Keep the tone warm, encouraging, and simple. Avoid jargon, or explain it when you use it.

Copy-and-paste prompt: solo or variant ideas

I play [name of game] and want to try a solo or two-player variant, or a house rule to make the game faster or friendlier. Please suggest:

  1. One simple variant or house rule
  2. How it changes the game
  3. What to watch out for (what could go wrong)

Mark all ideas as suggestions only, and note that players should agree on any house rules before starting.

Setup and teardown checklist

You can ask AI to help you build one, then print it and keep it in the game box:

  • Unpack all components and check them against the rulebook list
  • Set up the board and place starting pieces/tokens
  • Shuffle and deal cards or tiles
  • Give each player their starting resources or player board
  • Decide who goes first
  • Play!
  • Return all components to their bags or trays
  • Record scores if you want to track them over multiple games

Ask AI: Please make a setup and teardown checklist for [name of game], in simple checkbox form.

Scorekeeping and tracking help

AI can help you design a simple scoresheet or tracking table for a game that doesn't come with one. Ask:

Please design a simple score sheet for [name of game]. List what to track each round (points, resources, milestones) and suggest a clean table layout I can draw on paper.

Privacy reminder for board games: board games are social. Never paste real names, contact info, or private details about the people you play with into an AI chat. Refer to "Player 1," "Player 2," or use nicknames only. See the Privacy & Safety page.

Printable worksheet

The session worksheet works for board games too — use the "Rules to Look Up After the Session" section for rule clarifications and the "Session Summary" to note scores or house rules you want to remember next time.

What AI is NOT for board games

  • AI is not an official referee. The rulebook and the group's agreement decide how to play.
  • AI cannot see your physical board, cards, or pieces. Describe the situation yourself if you want help.
  • Do not paste copyrighted rulebook text into an AI chatbot. Summarize in your own words instead.
