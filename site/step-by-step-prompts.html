Step-by-Step Prompts

These prompts are ready to copy and paste. Replace the bracketed parts with your own details, then send them to your AI chatbot.

Character prompts

New character (story first)

I am making a character for a tabletop roleplaying game. I do not have the rulebook in front of me, so please do not quote exact rules or numbers.

Please give me a character concept with:

  1. A name
  2. A short personality description (2-3 sentences)
  3. A background or history (1 paragraph)
  4. Two or three things they are good at
  5. One flaw or fear
  6. One goal they want to achieve

Keep the tone friendly and the language simple. Do not include copyrighted stat blocks or game mechanics.

Describe your own character

I have a character concept: [paste your idea]. Please write a one-page character summary I can hand to my gamemaster. Include appearance, personality, background, strengths, flaws, and goals. Use plain language and do not invent game mechanics or quote rules.

Campaign prompts

Summarize a session

I will paste notes from a tabletop game session. Please write a short summary with:

  1. What happened (3-5 bullet points)
  2. Important NPCs the party met and what they wanted
  3. Clues, items, or promises the party now has
  4. Questions to answer next session
  5. Suggestions for what could happen next (mark these clearly as AI ideas)

Keep it under 300 words and use simple language. Here are my notes:

[paste your session notes here]

Organize messy notes

I have messy campaign notes below. Please organize them into:

  1. People (NPCs and players' characters)
  2. Places
  3. Events that happened
  4. Open questions and plot hooks

Use simple headings and bullet points. Do not invent new facts. If something is unclear, mark it with [unclear].

[paste your messy notes here]

NPC prompts

One NPC

Please create one NPC for a tabletop roleplaying game. Give me:

  1. Name
  2. Role or job
  3. One sentence describing what they look like
  4. Personality (2-3 sentences)
  5. What they want
  6. One secret they are keeping (optional, mark it clearly)
  7. One way the players could befriend or upset them

Keep the tone appropriate for a [fantasy / sci-fi / horror / other] game. Use plain language. Do not copy any copyrighted characters.

Three NPCs at once

Please create three different NPCs for a [setting]. Make them different from each other in job, personality, and goals. For each, give a name, one-line appearance, personality, and what they want. Keep descriptions short (under 60 words each) and language simple.

Rule prompts

Explain a rule

I play [name of game]. Please explain the rule for [specific rule] in plain language, as if I am playing for the first time. Include what it does, when to use it, and a simple example with made-up numbers. Do not quote the rulebook word for word. Mark anything you are unsure about with [unsure].

Compare two rules

In [name of game], what is the difference between [rule A] and [rule B]? Give a short, plain-language comparison with one example for each. Tell me if you are not certain.

Session prep prompts

Plan a session

I am preparing for a tabletop game session. My players are playing [briefly describe the characters]. The story so far: [1-2 sentences]. Please suggest:

  1. One scene or encounter for the session
  2. One NPC who could appear (name and one-line personality)
  3. One possible complication or twist
  4. One question to ask the players at the start

Keep it short, simple, and flexible. Mark all ideas as suggestions only.

Prep one encounter

Please design one simple encounter for [name of game]. Give me where it happens, who or what is involved, what could go well, what could go wrong, and how to end it either way. Use plain language and keep it under 200 words.

How to write your own prompts

  1. Say what you want (one thing at a time works best).
  2. Add constraints (length, tone, "plain language," "no copyrighted material").
  3. Ask it to mark uncertainty ([unsure]) so you know what to double-check.
  4. Paste your own notes instead of asking the AI to invent everything.

Remember: AI is a helper, not the gamemaster. You decide what goes at the table.
